# AI Usage Log

I leaned on Claude (for coding) and Gemini (for research) throughout this assessment, with Claude writing about 75% of the Python boilerplate. It was a massive time-saver when I gave it strict, narrow instructions, but I quickly learned it struggles with restraint and context. Instead of auditing the intern's code, it initially wanted to rewrite the whole thing from scratch, and it kept trying to "fix" the grapheme counting method without realizing we were evaluating a strategic design choice, not a syntax error. It's a great executor, but I had to actively manage it to keep the experiments grounded and focused on isolating one variable at a time.

I also found myself constantly fact-checking its interpretation of the data. In Part A, Claude confidently declared Telugu the "worst" language for tok/byte because it sorted the column backward, completely missing that a higher ratio means worse compression (making Hindi the actual outlier). In Part B, it tried to lazily wave away a 24% gap in the goodput calculations as just "wait time," forcing me to push back until we identified the actual statistical cause behind the discrepancy. Keeping the raw CSVs open to manually verify its claims was essential. Ultimately, AI was a fantastic, high-speed pair of hands for running calculations, but the actual engineering judgments like picking the tok/sentence metric or designing the Part C bootstrap strategy had to come from me.
